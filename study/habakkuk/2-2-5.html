Commentator Insights

Additional perspectives for Habakkuk 2:2–5. These are secondary explanations, separate from Bible Study 2.2’s fourteen modules and from Option 4’s connections between Scriptures.

John Calvin — Habakkuk 2:2–3

Calvin emphasizes that the written message serves others, not only the prophet. He pictures clear lettering that can be readily read. This is his explanation of the reading-and-running clause; the module study acknowledges other readings rather than presenting his reconstruction as a detail expressly stated in the verse.

On waiting, Calvin writes, “room must be given for hope.” He argues that God’s appointed fulfillment must not be measured by our demand for an immediate result. This helps the fellowship discuss patience while preserving the difference between a commentator’s explanation and Scripture itself.

Read Calvin’s commentary on Habakkuk 2:2–3

Matthew Henry — Habakkuk 2:4

Henry contrasts proud refusal to depend on God with faithful reliance during the waiting. He also identifies the later uses in Romans, Galatians, and Hebrews. His discussion helps connect confidence in God’s promise with continuing faithfulness, but his extended pastoral observations should not be mistaken for additional words in Habakkuk.

Matthew Henry — Habakkuk 2:5

Henry describes the conqueror’s discontent: “his ambition is his perpetual uneasiness.” He understands the gathering of peoples as the outward expression of an appetite that cannot accept enough. This is useful for examining ambition’s cost to others, while a modern application still needs to respect the original imperial setting.
