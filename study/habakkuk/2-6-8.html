Commentator Insights

Additional perspectives on Habakkuk 2:6–8, separate from the fourteen Bible Study 2.2 modules and from Option 4’s connections between Scriptures.

Matthew Henry — Rightful gain and its reversal

Henry writes, “No more of what we have is to be reckoned ours than what we came honestly by.” His point is that possession alone does not establish rightful ownership. In his treatment of verses 7–8, he also connects the aggressor’s coming loss with what it took from others.

Henry discusses the older thick-clay wording. That historical explanation should be distinguished from the active ESV’s pledges and from the lexical questions considered in WORDS and ALTERNATIVES. His pastoral explanation is secondary commentary, not additional Scripture.

Read Matthew Henry on Habakkuk 2:5–14

John Calvin — The nations answer the conqueror

Calvin explains verse 6 as a transition from the description of proud, consuming cruelty to its punishment. The peoples who were collected under the conqueror’s power become the people who taunt him. His interpretation helps trace the reversal between verses 5 and 6.

Calvin also distinguishes the forecast of taunting from a general rule about what believers should do to their enemies. His explanation should be tested against the oracle’s context; it must not turn ridicule into an unrestricted Christian duty.

Read Calvin on Habakkuk 2:6

Richard D. Patterson — The first woe’s structure

In his exegetical commentary published by Bible.org, Patterson presents the first woe as accusation, threat, and grounds for condemnation. He relates verse 6 to unjust accumulation, verse 7 to the reversal of the oppressor’s apparent security, and verse 8 to the plunder and violence explaining the judgment.

This makes the flow easier to follow while preserving a distinction between the verse’s stated outcome and details a commentator proposes for its imagery or historical setting. The source is commentary about the text; it does not replace Option 4’s classification of relationships between Scriptures.
