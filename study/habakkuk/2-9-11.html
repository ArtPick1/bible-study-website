Commentator Insights

Additional perspectives on Habakkuk 2:9–11, separate from all fourteen Bible Study 2.2 modules and from Option 4’s biblical connections.

Matthew Henry — provision does not excuse injustice

In his discussion of verses 9–11, Henry distinguishes lawful gain that can benefit a household from wealth obtained through fraud and injustice. He also observes that concern for safety can become an excuse for covetous ambition. The claimed benefit to the family does not change the moral quality of the means.

Connection to the passage: verse 9 names the intended security, while verse 10 exposes the disgrace actually brought upon the house. Henry’s comments help apply that reversal to household responsibility; they are a commentator’s explanation, not additional words of Scripture.

Matthew Henry, complete commentary on Habakkuk 2, verses 9–11

Richard D. Patterson — the plotting oppressor is denounced

Patterson treats the second woe as an exposure of the conqueror’s schemes and the judgment that answers them. He relates the high nest to the ambition for an unassailable establishment and notes that the house’s own materials supply the final accusing image.

Connection to the passage: the gain, the plan, and the witnesses belong to one argument. His additional notes also distinguish the life/self translation issue in verse 10 and the uncertain technical specification of the timber term in verse 11. These details refine the reading without overturning its central charge.

Richard D. Patterson, Habakkuk, section c: the second woe, 2:9–11

Keep the sources in their proper place

These are attributed summaries, not verbatim quotations. They are separate from Option 4, which examines connections within Scripture. Evaluate every commentator’s explanation by the actual words, immediate context, and wider biblical teaching. A useful observation does not become inspired text because it comes from a respected interpreter.
